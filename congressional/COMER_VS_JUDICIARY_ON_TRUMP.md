# What the Epstein files actually say about Trump

*Two House committees read the same files and reached opposite conclusions. Both claim more than the record supports.*

*By Rye · Document Zero · November 2026*

In October, House Judiciary Committee Democrats said the Epstein files show Donald Trump took part in Jeffrey Epstein's abuse. Two weeks later, the House Oversight Committee's Republican majority said the facts "never once led" to him. Both drew largely on the same released records, though each committee also had material the public doesn't: Judiciary's members read some documents unredacted, and each committee held its own interviews. I run [a searchable database][llms] of those records, so I went back to them.

One FBI interview shows the problem with both reports. In 2019, Palm Beach's former police chief told agents that Trump had called him about Epstein: "thank goodness you're stopping him, everyone has known he's been doing this." Trump, he said, "was one of the very first people to call when people found out that they were investigating EPSTEIN" ([FBI interview][1249718]). That's hard to square with what Trump told reporters in July 2019, when asked about Epstein "molesting young women, underaged women": "No, I had no idea" ([White House transcript][noidea]). It also shows Trump calling the police about Epstein, in 2006 by the chief's later account, which is evidence against a cover-up at that moment, though not proof of what he did or knew earlier. The Democrats' report quotes it secondhand, from a news story. The Republicans' report never mentions it.

That pattern runs through both reports. Each highlights the records that support its conclusion and leaves important contrary evidence unexplained. Both go further than the publicly documented investigation can justify. The FBI did interview some of these women, but none of the accounts has been tested in court or carried to a conclusion. Comer's committee called none of the eleven women Judiciary writes about, and the Justice Department didn't put its own Trump records to Maxwell. The basic question remains unanswered: which witnesses were questioned about which allegations, what corroboration was sought, and what happened to the leads investigators received?

*Disclosure: the Democrats' report cites [one of my earlier posts][dz98]. Nothing here relies on it. Survivors are named only where they have identified themselves publicly; everyone else goes by the reports' pseudonyms. FBI records whose contents could identify a survivor are described but not linked or numbered.*

## The short version

- **The Democrats overclaim.** The two women they lean on hardest, both witnesses at Ghislaine Maxwell's trial, told the FBI Trump had not abused them. In the FBI's notes, one "was not a vic of Trump"; for the other, "Nothing happened between [her] and TRUMP." Those are statements about their own encounters, not about what Trump knew or did elsewhere. The report prints neither. Its "Katie Johnson" record is a note of her lawyer's phone call to an FBI tip line, filed as an "ASSESSMENT ZERO FILE," a file for tips that don't open a case. And the transcript of the Justice Department's 2025 interview with Maxwell doesn't show officials silencing her about Trump. It shows them never confronting her with the Trump-related allegations and encounters in DOJ's own files.
- **The Republicans leave things out.** Their proof that witnesses cleared Trump is a string of "I don't know"s from people who weren't in a position to know. Epstein's longtime assistant testified that she put Epstein and Trump on the phone "maybe once a quarter," and the page the report cites stops two lines before she confirms it. The report says it found no documents implicating Trump, but never mentions the ones that contain allegations. It cites a newspaper for Trump banning Epstein from Mar-a-Lago and ignores the FBI interview above.
- **What holds up:** A long friendship. At least eight flights on Epstein's plane. Phone messages from Trump found in Epstein's house in 2005. And a Justice Department that pulled together what its files said about Trump just before it interviewed Maxwell, then didn't put any of it to her.

That Trump took part in Epstein's abuse is unproven. That he has been cleared is also unproven. Neither has been tested: no allegation has been carried to a conclusion, and the one witness DOJ questioned, days after its own staff compiled Trump material, wasn't confronted with it.

## The two reports

House Judiciary Committee Democrats released [*The Case Against Donald Trump Emerging in the Epstein Files*][jud] on October 26, 2026, eight days before the midterms. It argues that Trump was "an active participant in Jeffrey Epstein's sexual abuse of young women," that DOJ is covering it up ([Jud. p.2][j2]), and that a special counsel is needed ([Jud. p.52][j51]).

The House Oversight Committee's Republican majority released its [137-page report][com] on November 10. Most of it covers Epstein's recruitment network, the 2008 non-prosecution agreement, his bankers and his death. One subsection, V.C, is titled "Democrats Focused All of Their Efforts on President Trump––Not on Justice for Survivors." It says "the facts never once led to President Trump" ([Comer p.126][c126]).

Comer's report is not a rebuttal of Judiciary's. It never cites it. V.C attacks Oversight Democrats' conduct in 2025 and 2026, and cites nothing later than June 2026. Read Comer as the majority's standing position, published after the Judiciary report without engaging it.

Below, "Judiciary" means the Democrats' report and "Comer" means the Republicans' report, after the committee's chairman, James Comer. Page numbers are the reports' printed page numbers, and each links to that page.

## Where the Democrats' report outruns its evidence

### Its two best witnesses told the FBI Trump had not abused them

Judiciary leans hardest on two women who testified against Ghislaine Maxwell under the pseudonyms "Jane" and "Kate." Prosecutors found them credible enough to put on the stand, and the report treats that as a credential for their accounts of Trump ([Jud. p.3][j3]).

**Jane.** At 14, at Mar-a-Lago, Epstein introduced her to Trump with the line "This is a good one, huh," and Trump said "Yes" ([Jud. p.21][j21]; [FBI slide deck][164939], slide 18). The account is firsthand: her 2019 FBI interview records Epstein's line ([FBI interview, 2019][158473], p.3). But in a February 2020 interview, asked whether she knew of "a DONALD TRUMP allegation of sexual abuse," she "stated no." The FBI agent then wrote: "There was some saying that she was a victim of TRUMP but this was not true" ([FBI interview, 2020][158500], p.14). An FBI email of July 22, 2025, compiled two days before the Maxwell interview, lists both her Mar-a-Lago account and a later FBI record in which she "states she was not a vic of Trump" ([FBI email, July 22, 2025][151753]). The Judiciary report prints the first and not the second. **ESTABLISHED.**

**Kate.** Judiciary says Maxwell "presented" Kate to Trump "with the clear understanding that she was available for sex" ([Jud. p.27][j27]). Her 2021 FBI interview reads: "[She] never dated DONALD TRUMP … MAXWELL presented [her] to TRUMP and they had a conversation for approximately 20 minutes … Nothing happened between [her] and TRUMP. By the things that MAXWELL said, it was made clear that [she] was available" ([FBI interview, 2021][158676], p.7). The same page adds that Maxwell said "Oh I think he likes you. Aren't you lucky," and that "It was set up very much like how MAXWELL introduced [her] to EPSTEIN." Judiciary also quotes, without a source, a 2025 statement by Kate that Maxwell introduced her "with a clear message of my being with him in the same way that she had trafficked me" ([Jud. p.27][j27]). The statement comes from a BBC *Newsnight* interview, and in the same interview she said: "at no time did President Trump behave with any impropriety with me" (both statements are quoted from the *Newsnight* interview in [Sen. Sheldon Whitehouse's published floor remarks][whitehouse] and in OK! magazine's account, [republished here][kateokbbc]; I could not obtain the BBC original). So the report's reading of Maxwell's intent has support from Kate herself. What it leaves out is the rest of what she said, to the FBI ("never dated," "Nothing happened") and to the BBC. Judiciary concedes only that she "does not explicitly claim any misconduct by Trump." **ESTABLISHED.**

The two accounts establish different things. Jane described Epstein introducing her to Trump at 14 with a suggestive remark. Kate described Maxwell presenting her to Trump in a way that made clear she was available. Neither woman told the FBI that Trump abused her. That doesn't answer what Trump knew about Epstein or Maxwell, or whether he did anything with anyone else.

### Its five "direct" accusers: what the public record shows

The report says five women "directly accused Trump of raping or sexually assaulting them" ([Jud. p.3][j3]). Here is what the released record shows for each. The FBI may hold more that hasn't been released; this is an assessment of what's public.

- **Katie Johnson.** The report says she "gave FBI a detailed account" ([Jud. p.3][j3]) and refers to "the FBI case agent responsible for this interview" ([Jud. p.25][j25]). The record is an FBI Complaint Form: "Receipt Method: Telephone," received July 4, 2016, from her attorney, with case ID "ASSESSMENT ZERO FILE" ([FBI complaint form][1683870], p.1). In the records I reviewed, no FBI interview with her was located, and the complaint form is not one. A zero-file classification doesn't by itself rule out later work under another file number. Her civil suits were withdrawn twice in 2016, a fact the report confines to a footnote headline. The tip exists. No record I found shows the FBI took her account.
- **Jane Doe 4.** Judiciary says the FBI interviewed her in 2019 and places her abuse by Epstein in South Carolina in 1983, but the only FBI document it cites is a secondhand tip: a 2025 log in which a caller reports what a friend told her, placing the Trump incident "approximately 35 years ago in NJ" ([Jud. p.31][j31]; tip not linked, because it exposes a name). The release also holds the FBI's own summary of an in-person interview, dated Aug. 7, 2019 (not linked or numbered here, to protect her). She told agents that when she was 13 to 15, Epstein took her to "either New York or New Jersey," where she was "introduced to someone with money, money... It was Donald Trump." She said Trump forced her head down, she "bit" him, and he "struck" her. She said she had "two additional interactions with TRUMP" but asked to move on. That matches the core of the tip and of the FBI's "Prominent Names" slide: an introduction by Epstein, forced oral contact, a bite, a blow. The details differ: the tip has her laughing and puts the event about 35 years back, around 1990, while the interview points to the early 1980s. That the tip's "friend" is this woman is inferred, not documented. Judiciary doesn't cite this firsthand record, which is stronger than the tip it does cite. It is still one person's account to the FBI, never tested, and the report itself says it "has not been able to independently verify" her claims ([Jud. p.31][j31]). Its suggestion that DOJ gave her unredacted interview notes to a conservative journalist rests on footnote 98, which cites my own post. That is not independent corroboration.
- **Beatrice Keul.** The rape allegation appears only in interviews with committee staff. Footnote 49 says that until now she had chosen to say she "talked 'Trump down'" ([Jud. p.16][j16]). Her October 2024 press interviews described an attempted assault, not a completed one ([The Independent][indep], cited by Judiciary at fn.53). Late disclosure is common among survivors and doesn't make the account false. It does mean the most serious allegation in the report has one venue, staff interviews, while the executive summary says she "credibly alleges" it without qualification.
- **Stacey Williams.** An on-record account of groping in Trump Tower in 1993. Three friends confirmed to CNN that she told them about it in 2006, 2015 and 2018 ([CNN][cnnwilliams]; [Jud. p.19][j19]). Those friends corroborate that she told people over the years, not the assault itself. By her account the only other person present was Epstein, who is dead. It is still the best supported of the five, and it is not in the DOJ files.
- **Jill Harth.** A 1997 federal civil complaint, withdrawn as part of a related settlement. The report dates the incident to 1994 on page 3 and to 1993 on pages 20–21.

None of the five alleged acts has independent corroboration, such as a witness or a record of the act itself. Two accounts have corroboration that the woman told others years ago (Williams, and probably Jane Doe 4 through the 2025 tip). None has been tested in court. Only one, Jane Doe 4's, shows an FBI interview. The FBI put her account on a 2025 "Prominent Names" slide ([FBI slide deck][164939], slide 18), and in August 2025, after the tip, sent a lead to its Washington office to interview the caller's friend (tip not linked, as above). I found no record of the outcome. Of the remaining six women in the report's count of eleven, three (a woman named in Harth's account, and Jane Does 5 and 6) "have not come forward as survivors," in the report's own words, and have not publicly alleged that they were victims ([Jud. p.32][j32]). Counting them toward "11 women who tie Donald Trump to Epstein's sex trafficking operation" inflates the number.

### The Blanche transcript doesn't say what the report says

Judiciary says Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, interviewing Maxwell in July 2025, "directed Maxwell not to discuss" Jane on "at least three" occasions ([Jud. p.5][j5]), and that "the whole point of the conversation was … to create a conspiracy of silence" about Trump ([Jud. p.23][j23]). It floats witness tampering and obstruction under 18 U.S.C. §§1512 and 1505.

The count of three is right ([Maxwell transcript, Day 2][d2], tr. pp.292–302). The characterization is not.

- The subject of all three redirects is whether Maxwell knew girls were underage, and her attempts to dispute individual accusers' trial testimony.
- Trump isn't mentioned in those pages. He is mentioned twice in all of Day 2.
- Between "I don't want to get into … he said, she said" and "Let's not talk about individuals," Maxwell kept arguing that Jane's testimony was wrong ("I don't believe he met her 'til she was 16").

On Day 1, Blanche did ask about Trump. Did she ever see him receive a massage? Did she ever hear he "had done anything inappropriate with masseuses"? Maxwell: "Absolutely never," and "he was a gentleman in all respects" ([Maxwell transcript, Day 1][d1], tr. pp.106–108). He also asked whether she remembered "ever recruiting a masseuse from Mar-a-Lago spa" to give Epstein a private massage. "I can't ever recollect doing that," she said (tr. pp.108–109).

What Blanche did *not* do is the stronger charge, and Judiciary half-makes it. He did not put any of the following to Maxwell, all of which were in DOJ's files:

- the Mar-a-Lago introduction of a 14-year-old
- the flights
- Kate's account that Maxwell "presented" her to Trump

At 3:17 a.m. that morning, an FBI tasking email had said: "Take these names and build out new spreadsheet w al[l] t[h]e derog on them. Trump …" ([FBI tasking email][1648955]). ("Derog" is FBI shorthand for derogatory, or damaging, information.) Two days earlier the FBI had compiled what the trial witnesses said about Trump ([FBI email, July 22, 2025][151753]). Whether any of it reached Blanche is unknown. That none of it was put to Maxwell is on the record.

The transcript does not establish Judiciary's theory that Blanche was silencing Maxwell to protect Trump. Section 1512 covers several offenses, including interfering with what a witness tells law enforcement, and §1505 covers obstructing pending agency or congressional proceedings; each requires a wrongful means, such as force, threats, intimidation, corrupt persuasion, misleading conduct or acting "corruptly," aimed at the testimony or proceeding. What the pages show is an interviewer steering away from disputes over individual accusers' testimony. The established failure is narrower: DOJ's second-ranking official did not put the documented allegations and encounters to the key witness. The record doesn't show what material he had been given.

### Specific errors

- **The Zampolli model "needing a visa."** Judiciary says Paolo Zampolli "arranged for her to receive a visa" ([Jud. p.14][j14]). Her FBI interview says she "was born in San Antonio, Texas" ([FBI interview][1245554], p.1). It also says she was recruited to Epstein through someone she met at a later job, after Zampolli had "let her go."
- **Maria Farmer's FBI reports "appear to be unlawfully omitted"** ([Jud. p.27][j27]). An FBI summary in the release describes a 1996 tip that matches her account and notes it went to a "zero file," a catch-all for tips that don't open a case ([FBI case update][165512]). Its 2006 entry is likely her sister's interview, not hers. Neither underlying record is in the release, and the summary doesn't mention Trump. Whether anything of Maria's was withheld is unproven. (Details in the appendix.)
- **The "mysterious" case number.** Judiciary says it is "unclear why Donald Trump would have been associated with" FBI case 31E-HQ-C1461253, which turned up when the FBI searched its case system for Trump and Epstein in 2016 ([Jud. p.25][j25]; [FBI complaint form][1683870], p.3). A document it cites in the same footnote answers the question. It is a November 2007 FBI headquarters memo under that number, in the Epstein child-prostitution file, which says: "Of particular interest on the message pads were calls from notable celebrities and public figures such as Donald Trump, David Copperfield, Sarah - Duchess of York, and Halle Barry, indicating the social status of Jeffrey Epstein" ([FBI memo, 2007][1246277], p.3). Trump is in the file as a caller. Nothing shows he was a subject of it.
- **"With Epstein's victims."** The executive summary says Trump "flew on his jet with Epstein's victims" ([Jud. p.2][j2]). The prosecutor's email it relies on says two flights carried women "who would be possible witnesses in a Maxwell case" ([prosecutor's email][28716]). A witness is not necessarily a victim.
- **The Melania introduction.** One sentence in the FBI's notes of a 2019 proffer, an interview in which a witness offers information to prosecutors: "EPSTEIN introduced MELANIA TRUMP to DONALD TRUMP" ([FBI proffer notes][159321], p.2). The witness worked for Epstein around 2004–06; the Trumps met in 1998 (at a party Zampolli hosted, per [the New York Times][nytzampolli] and [Jud. p.11][j11]). The sentence gives no basis for her knowledge, and Melania Trump has denied the account ([BBC][bbcmelania]; [Jud. p.13][j13]). Judiciary says the files "strongly suggest" it ([Jud. p.2][j2]).

## Where the Republicans' report ignores its evidence

### "Every witness" means witnesses who said "I don't know"

Comer says that "each time a witness was asked about President Trump, the witness responded either that they knew nothing" or "vehemently disagreed," and calls Trump's "lack of involvement … confirmed" ([Comer p.126][c126]). Footnote 754 gives twelve pin cites, meaning references to specific pages and lines of testimony. I checked all twelve witnesses' answers: eleven against transcripts (four of them machine transcripts of video) and Acosta against the committee majority's own published excerpts of his transcript (full table in the appendix).

Most of the answers are narrow. Witnesses say they had no knowledge of the Epstein–Trump relationship, recall no documents naming Trump, know of no Trump visits during their own employment, or saw nothing untoward. Those are different answers with different weight, but none is knowledge that Trump did nothing. Two speak from their own contact with Trump: Kellen describes a five-minute introduction, and Clinton a conversation in which, by the video transcript, Trump told him "we had some great times together over the years, but we fell out" over "a real estate deal." Neither describes the 1990s. Three of the twelve are officials; the rest knew Epstein mostly outside the 1992–97 window Judiciary writes about. None of Judiciary's eleven women was called, and neither was any Mar-a-Lago employee, 1990s pageant witness or Palm Beach police witness ([Comer pp.8–9][c8]). "To their knowledge" carries the whole finding ([Comer p.128][c128]).

The one Comer witness who described ongoing Epstein–Trump contact is the one Comer cuts short. Lesley Groff, Epstein's assistant from 2001, testified: "I just merely would connect Mr. Epstein with Mr. Trump … on the phone." How often? "It was just sporadic. Every so often … Maybe once a quarter. I don't know." Asked whether it "could have been multiple times a year," she said "Yes," and added, "I don't really recall when they stopped talking" ([Groff tr. 147–148, 155][groff]). Comer's pin cite is 156:4–10. The next lines, 11–14, are a question about her having "connected them multiple times a year by phone" and her answer, "Yes." Elsewhere Comer calls Groff's testimony "not credible" ([Comer p.2][c2]).

On the page before Lutnick's cited "No," his counsel blocked a question about whether he had discussed Epstein with the President: "We're not going to violate executive privilege" ([Lutnick tr. 92][lutnick]).

### "No documents" depends on the meaning of "implicate"

Comer says its review of Estate, bank, Virgin Islands and DOJ records found no documents "that would implicate President Trump in any criminal activity" ([Comer p.128][c128]). It mentions none of the 24 primary documents Judiciary cites. Each EFTA-numbered one was in DOJ's public release by January 30, 2026, more than nine months before Comer published. The two HOUSE_OVERSIGHT documents came from the Estate's production to Comer's own committee.

If "implicate" means "prove," Comer's sentence survives: no document is a finding, a charge or a corroborated firsthand account of a crime by Trump. If it means "contain an allegation," the sentence is false. The release includes the 2016 complaint form alleging child rape ([FBI complaint form][1683870]) and an FBI slide listing two Trump items under "PROMINENT NAMES" ([FBI slide deck][164939], slide 18). A report that holds Epstein's staff to a "red flags" standard owed readers at least a sentence saying these documents exist and why it discounts them.

### The Mar-a-Lago ban: a newspaper over an FBI file

There are several documented accounts of whether Trump ever threw Epstein out of Mar-a-Lago, and they conflict. Comer says Trump "reportedly banned Epstein from Mar-a-Lago" after a spa-employee complaint, citing the [*Wall Street Journal*][wsj] ([Comer p.128][c128]). The *Journal* reported that in 2003, after an 18-year-old spa employee complained, a manager faxed Trump and Trump said to kick Epstein out ([summary][mediaitewsj]; [another][rawwsj]). It ignores two records that bear on that claim, one on each side.

- **For the ban:** A 2019 FBI interview of the former Palm Beach police chief records that "DONALD TRUMP told [him] that he threw EPSTEIN out of his club" ([FBI interview][1249718], p.3). Barr testified to Comer's own committee that Trump told him in 2019 he had "pushed him out of Mar-a-Lago" ([Barr tr. 46][barr]). Kellen told the committee she understood Trump "banned him from going because he had hit on, like, a member's daughter or something along those lines" ([Kellen tr. 56][kellentr]), a version that matches neither the *Journal*'s nor Trump's.
- **Against it:** An October 2009 email from Epstein's lawyer summarizing a 20-minute call with Trump, Trump's lawyer Alan Garten, and survivors' attorney Brad Edwards ([the redacted email][740636]). DOJ's public copy shows the setup line, then one black box over the entire body. Judiciary, whose Members read it unredacted, says Trump stated Epstein "was never asked to leave" and that the club manager "confirmed" it, and reprints a recreation of the full email ([Jud. pp.38–40][j38]; DOJ's redacted copy: [the redacted email][740636]). Rep. Dan Goldman displayed the same text on the House floor in March 2026 ([Daily Beast][dbeast]). It also has Trump on the plane question: "I've been on a lot of planes may have been on his plane. No young girls on plane."

Epstein himself gave a third version in 2019: "trump said he asked me to resign, never a member ever" ([Epstein to Wolff, 2019][1028620]). So "kicked him out" was not invented in 2019 or 2025, and Judiciary's reproduction of the 2009 email attributes "never asked to leave" to Trump that year. The public copy is redacted, so that reproduction can't be checked word for word. Judiciary sets out both possibilities and argues each is damaging ([Jud. p.40][j40]). Comer presents only the ban. One date matters: Epstein recruited Virginia Giuffre from the Mar-a-Lago spa in 2000, and Trump praised Epstein in 2002 and signed the birthday book in 2003. A ban over Giuffre in 2000 is hard to square with that. A ban after a later incident is not. Neither version is proven.

DOJ's stated reason for blacking out the whole email, given by Blanche at a public hearing, was "a privileged communication between counsel" ([MS NOW][msnow]). Judiciary answers that a call including Trump, his lawyer and the survivors' attorney was not privileged in the first place, and that Epstein then forwarded it to Maxwell, a non-lawyer ([Jud. fn.121][j39]). Forwarding a privileged message to a third party can waive privilege, though the outcome depends on who held the privilege and on the circumstances. The stronger point is that DOJ has not explained why the forwarding didn't waive protection, or why the entire body, rather than any legal advice in it, had to be blacked out.

### Lutnick: "virtually nonexistent"

Comer reports that it "confirmed a meeting in Epstein's foyer taking place in 2011 and a 2012 lunch with Lutnick's family on Little St. James" and still accepts Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's description of his contact with Epstein as "virtually nonexistent" ([Comer p.82][c82]; [Lutnick tr. p.9][lutnick]). The emails behind the lunch:

- A December 2012 email in the name "Lutnick, Howard": "Hi Jeff, We are landing in St. Thomas early Saturday … Does Sunday evening for dinner sound good?" followed by "OK, lunch on Sunday. See you then." The lunch was on Little St. James ([emails][398726]).

Lutnick testified that mail in that form was handled, and sometimes answered in his name, by his assistant ([tr. 22][lutnick]), so whether he wrote them himself is unproven. Comer tests claimed ignorance from Groff, Indyke and Kahn against documents and rejects it. For a Cabinet secretary, it reports the island lunch and adopts his "virtually nonexistent" anyway. The standard differs.

### "Championed transparency"

Section V is titled "President Trump and the 119th Congress Championed Transparency." Its own subsection is titled "The DOJ Failed to Meet Statutory Requirements Despite Efforts to Comply" ([Comer p.117][c117]). The timeline it recites:

- the July 2025 memo declaring "no further disclosure would be appropriate or warranted"
- a subpoena the committee had to issue
- the missed December 19, 2025 deadline
- about 3.5 million pages released of about 6 million collected
- about 200,000 withheld or redacted on common-law privileges the statute does not list
- litigation, a court order and an inspector-general review

The section title is contradicted by the facts in the section.

### The Maxwell interview is missing entirely

Comer's stated subject is "the alleged mismanagement of the federal government's investigation." It reports that Maxwell is at FPC Bryan and that she took the Fifth before the committee ([Comer p.133][c133]). It never mentions how she got to Bryan: Blanche's July 24–25, 2025 interview ([Day 1][d1], [Day 2][d2]), followed by her transfer to the minimum-security camp. Comer's third recommendation is that DOJ "interview or reinterview key witnesses" ([Comer p.135][c135]). The most important witness was interviewed by DOJ's second-ranking official, asked nothing drawn from DOJ's own Trump records, moved, and then refused to answer Comer.

The police chief recalls Trump saying, in 2006 by his later account, that "everyone has known he's been doing this," and Epstein claimed Trump "knew about the girls." ## What the record does show

Strip away both reports' framing and the primary record still supports more than Comer admits and less than Judiciary claims.

**The police chief's file cuts both ways.** The same 2019 FBI interview ([FBI interview][1249718], pp.3–4) records the following about Trump:

- He told the chief he "threw EPSTEIN out of his club."
- He called police to say "thank goodness you're stopping him, everyone has known he's been doing this."
- He said "he was around EPSTEIN once when teenagers were present and TRUMP 'got the hell out of there.'"
- He said "MAXWELL was EPSTEIN's operative," called her "evil" and told police "to focus on her."
- He "was one of the very first people to call when people found out that they were investigating EPSTEIN."

The interview does not date the call; the chief later told the Miami Herald it was in July 2006 ([as reported][herald2006]). Placed beside Trump's July 2019 "I had no idea" ([White House transcript][noidea]; [Jud. p.41][j41]), it is an FBI record, through the chief's recollection, that contradicts that public denial. Judiciary makes the same argument. It is also among the records most favorable to Trump: in 2006 he was urging police on, not shielding Epstein. It doesn't establish what he knew or did before then. Judiciary quotes one sentence of it, through ABC. Comer ignores it.

**The calls: documented contact, uncertain timing.** The FBI's 2007 memo records calls from Trump on the message pads Palm Beach police obtained from Epstein's house in 2005 ([FBI memo, 2007][1246277], p.3). The Palm Beach Post, which obtained the slips from the state attorney's files, reported two messages from Trump, each giving only his phone number. They are undated but sit among slips dated Nov. 11–20, 2004, and the Nov. 15 auction of the Palm Beach house the two men were bidding on falls in that window ([Palm Beach Post][pbpslips]). So the documented calls may be about the property fight rather than a continuing friendship. Groff describes placing calls from 2001 on. Both reports accept a falling-out over that 2004 deal. The friendship did not end in 2000. When it ended is unknown.

**Eight flights.** A January 2020 prosecutor's email: Trump "is listed as a passenger on at least eight flights between 1993 and 1996, including at least four flights on which Maxwell was also present." On one, "he and Epstein are the only two listed passengers." On another, "the only three passengers are Epstein, Trump, and then-20-year-old [redacted]." Marla Maples, Tiffany and Eric were aboard on others ([prosecutor's email][28716]). Trump's 2024 statement that he "was never on Epstein's plane" is false.

**Epstein's emails are evidence of what Epstein said.** "Of course he knew about the girls as he asked ghislaine to stop" ([Epstein to Wolff, 2019][1028620]) comes in an email that opens with Giuffre working at Mar-a-Lago and "trump said he asked me to resign, never a member ever." Read whole, Epstein appears to be claiming that Trump knew Maxwell was recruiting at his club and objected, which is close to Trump's own account. The email doesn't say what Maxwell was asked to stop, and it can't show what Trump actually knew, whether he knew of abuse, or whether Epstein's claim is true. "That dog that hasn't barked is trump.. virignia spent hours at my house with him" ([Epstein to Maxwell, 2011][654425]) is Epstein writing privately to Maxwell. Giuffre testified in a 2016 deposition that she had never been in Trump's and Epstein's presence together ([excerpt][giuffre]), so one of them is wrong.

The best reason to discount Epstein's later statements about Trump is in the file and in neither report. In December 2015, Michael Wolff advised Epstein about Trump: "I think you should let him hang himself … that gives you a valuable PR and political currency … or, if it really looks like he could win, you could save him, generating a debt" ([Wolff to Epstein, 2015][32229]). That gives a possible motive for what Epstein said about Trump from then on, including the 2019 email to Wolff. It doesn't show that any particular statement was strategic. The 2011 email to Maxwell predates it.

**What the FBI found, and what it said in public.** In March 2025, FBI New York relayed to Deputy Director Bongino an SDNY prosecutor's account that reviews of all videos, images and case files revealed no evidence "that anyone other than Epstein and Maxwell participated in the sexual abuse of victims in this case" ([FBI email, March 2025][164742]). That is a finding about the seized media and files, not a test of every witness account. On July 10, 2025, in an email headed "Response to 07/10/2025 Director's call," an FBI official wrote: "Approximately 4-5 victims accused other adult men sexually abused them. There was not enough evidence to federally charge these individuals, so the cases were referred to local law enforcement" ([FBI email, July 10, 2025][164897]). Three days earlier, DOJ had told the public it "did not uncover evidence that could predicate an investigation against uncharged third parties" ([DOJ–FBI memo][dojmemo]; [Comer p.117][c117]).

**No evidence of a blanket removal of Trump's name.** "Trump" is visible across the duplicate copies of the documents above: the FBI's "Prominent Names" slide went out with every prominent man's name readable. That defeats the claim that his name was removed across the board. It doesn't rule out selective withholding. What is redacted is specific content, above all the 2009 email. A systematic pattern of protecting Trump is unproven. Careless exposure of survivors, which Comer concedes ([Comer p.124][c124]), is documented.

## Methodology

Both reports, the [epstein-data.com][llms] corpus documentation and my report protocol went to several independent AI research agents, each working through the same set of about forty questions. Nothing any of them produced was used unless I could confirm it against the primary record.

I then re-pulled every primary document quoted here through the corpus API, read it in full, and checked each quotation. I also checked the Groff, Barr, Lutnick and Bondi transcripts against the committee's published PDFs, and confirmed the DOJ response and the Goldman floor display through press reports. Where the agents disagreed with each other, the primary record decided.

Report page numbers are the printed numbers. Links jump to the matching PDF page. Corpus links go to the epstein-data.com viewer, and the [corpus API][llms] returns the same text.

## The Farmer records

Judiciary says Maria Farmer's 1996 and 2006 reports to the FBI "appear to be unlawfully omitted" ([Jud. p.27][j27]). An FBI update in the release summarizes both contacts. (An FD-71 is the FBI's form for logging a tip; an FD-302 is its standard write-up of an interview; a "1A" is an envelope of supporting material such as notes; a "zero file" is a catch-all file for tips that don't open a case.) For 1996: "This FD-71 was sent to a zero file." For 2006: the interview "was not memorialized in an FD-302, however a 1A containing 23 pages of handwritten notes was located" ([FBI case update][165512]). The 1996 entry matches Maria Farmer's account. The 2006 entry is likely not hers: the names are redacted, and Maxwell's lawyers later wrote that "Accuser-2" "was interviewed on November 14, 2006," the same date ([Maxwell filing][ogr2504], p.7), and presumed Accuser-2 to be Maria's sister, Annie Farmer ([same filing][ogr2504], p.22; [another filing][ogr11424], p.20). So the 2006 entry is likely Annie's, and the record doesn't show whether Maria spoke to the FBI in 2006. Neither the 1996 form nor the 2006 notes was located in the release, and the FBI's summary of them doesn't mention Trump. Whether anything of Maria's was withheld is unproven.
